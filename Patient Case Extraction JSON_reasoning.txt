# Patient Case Extraction: Reasoning-First Approach

## Primary Goal

Extract ONLY explicitly stated information from the uploaded patient case document using a Reasoning-First JSON approach to support ECHOWS-based clinical interviewing practice.

---

## Core Extraction Principles

1. Extract information **ONLY** from the uploaded document
2. Use direct quotes with exact language from the document
3. Provide a location citation for each quote (e.g., "paragraph 3")
4. Use "Information not provided in the document" for any missing data
5. Do not interpret, infer, or add information not explicitly stated
6. **CRITICAL:** Complete the "reasoning" field BEFORE extracting structured data
7. Document your thought process, ambiguities, and decision-making in reasoning fields

---

## Chain-of-Thought Processing Instructions

### Step 1: Initial Assessment
Start by completing the `extractionMetadata` section. Think through:
- What type of case is this?
- What sections are present?
- What challenges do you anticipate?
- What information appears complete vs. incomplete?

### Step 2: Section-by-Section Reasoning
For each major section (demographics, chief complaint, medical history, etc.):
1. **First**, read and complete the "reasoning" field
2. Use reasoning to note what's present vs. absent
3. Identify any ambiguities or interpretive challenges
4. Document your decision-making process
5. Flag information that might be inferred vs. explicitly stated
6. **Only then** populate the quote/location fields

### Step 3: Quality Verification
Complete the verification report with:
- Any contradictions found
- Ambiguities identified
- Quality control assessment

---

## Benefits of Reasoning-First Approach

- Reduces errors in complex or ambiguous cases
- Improves accuracy for hemodynamic data, medication dosing, and technical measurements
- Documents extraction decisions for quality review
- Helps identify contradictions and ambiguities before finalizing structure
- Provides transparency in how information was interpreted and categorized

## Reasoning Field Examples

### Example 1: Medication with Ambiguity
```
"reasoning": "Patient medications listed in paragraph 3 with specific dosing. Paragraph 5 mentions 'blood pressure medication' which appears to refer to the same Lisinopril mentioned earlier. Using the explicit medication name and dose from paragraph 3 as it's more specific. No other medications mentioned."
```

### Example 2: Contradictory Information
```
"reasoning": "Age stated as '42 years old' in paragraph 1 and described as 'early 40s' in paragraph 5. These are consistent but paragraph 1 is more specific. Using exact age from paragraph 1. Noting in verification report that age is mentioned in both locations with compatible descriptions."
```

### Example 3: Missing Information
```
"reasoning": "Document does not mention allergies anywhere. Medical history section in paragraph 4 lists past conditions and current medications but makes no reference to allergies, either to state presence or absence. Marking as 'Information not provided' rather than 'No known allergies' since absence of mention is not the same as explicit statement of no allergies."
```

---

## Important Notes

1. **Record direct quotes** from the clinician to support your scoring
2. **Maintain complete reasoning** before extracting each section
3. **Track criteria observations** with specific examples in real-time
4. **Base all evaluations** solely on demonstrated information in the current document
5. **Include reasoning for both** present and absent information
6. **Document interpretive choices** transparently
7. **Never reveal or skip** the reasoning process - it is essential for accuracy
